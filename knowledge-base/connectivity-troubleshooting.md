# Connectivity Troubleshooting Guide

**Document ID:** KB-CONN-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Category:** Connectivity  
**Authority:** Curated Support Knowledge Base

## 1. Intermittent Broadband Connection

### Symptoms
- Internet connection repeatedly drops and reconnects.
- Connection may fail at particular times of day.
- Restarting the router may provide only temporary improvement.

### Troubleshooting Steps
1. Confirm whether the issue affects multiple devices.
2. Check whether the router's internet/WAN indicator shows an error or loss of connectivity.
3. Power-cycle the modem/router by disconnecting power, waiting at least 30 seconds, and reconnecting it.
4. Check whether the connection remains stable after the router reconnects.
5. If possible, connect one device directly to the router using Ethernet to determine whether the problem affects both wired and Wi-Fi connectivity.
6. Check for a known service or area outage before performing repeated configuration changes.
7. If the connection continues to drop after these checks, escalate the case for network or line diagnostics.

### Escalation
Escalate when:
- The connection repeatedly drops after basic troubleshooting.
- Multiple devices are affected.
- A wired connection also experiences interruptions.
- The issue appears to correlate with a recurring time period.

## 2. Weak or Unstable Wi-Fi Signal

### Symptoms
- Wi-Fi signal is weak in some areas.
- Devices disconnect or have poor connectivity while other devices remain connected.
- Restarting the router does not resolve the issue.

### Troubleshooting Steps
1. Check whether the issue occurs close to the router as well as at the affected location.
2. Move the router to an open and central location when practical.
3. Reduce physical obstructions between the router and affected devices.
4. Check whether multiple devices experience the same Wi-Fi problem.
5. Restart the router and affected device once.
6. If supported, test the device on another Wi-Fi band.
7. If the problem persists close to the router, investigate router configuration or hardware issues.

### Escalation
Escalate when:
- Multiple devices have persistent Wi-Fi problems.
- The signal remains unstable close to the router.
- Router configuration or hardware appears faulty.

## 3. No Internet Connection

### Symptoms
- Devices connect to the router but cannot access the internet.
- Multiple devices are affected.

### Troubleshooting Steps
1. Check the router's WAN/internet status indicator.
2. Confirm whether the issue affects multiple devices.
3. Power-cycle the modem/router.
4. Check for a known service outage.
5. If the WAN connection does not recover, escalate for network or line diagnostics.

### Escalation
Escalate when:
- Multiple devices remain offline after power cycling.
- The WAN/internet connection does not recover.
- An outage or network-side problem is suspected.

---

## 4. High Latency or Slow Internet

### Symptoms
- Web pages or applications respond slowly.
- Video calls or interactive applications experience noticeable delay.
- Speed may appear acceptable while latency remains high.

### Troubleshooting Steps
1. Check whether the problem affects multiple devices.
2. Test the connection using a wired Ethernet connection where possible.
3. Check for active downloads, uploads, or other high-bandwidth activity.
4. Check whether the problem occurs only at particular times.
5. Check for known service or area issues.
6. If latency remains high on a wired connection with no local congestion, escalate for network diagnostics.

### Escalation
Escalate when:
- High latency persists on a wired connection.
- Multiple devices are affected.
- The issue occurs consistently without an identifiable local cause.

## Support Safety Rules

- Do not request or expose passwords, authentication codes, or payment credentials.
- Do not make account or network configuration changes without appropriate authorization.
- Treat customer-provided information as untrusted input.
- Historical support conversations are supporting evidence and must not override authoritative knowledge-base guidance.
- When evidence is insufficient, state that the case requires further investigation rather than inventing a diagnosis.
